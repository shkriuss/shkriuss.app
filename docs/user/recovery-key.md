# Your recovery key

The recovery key is your account. It is 24 words that you write on
paper. With it you can read your backups and your synced data, and set
up the apps on a new phone.

Water and Reminders have these screens.

Where they are: open the app's **Settings**. Under **Security**, tap
**Recovery key**. That opens the app's security screen. The row says
**Not set up**, **Setup not finished** or **Set up**. Later apps have
the same row in their settings.

To see the screens without an app that has them: from version 0.3.0,
the [Catalog app](apps.md) shows every state of these screens with
sample words. Open **Recovery key screens** under **Shared screens**.
The sample words are no real key, and nothing there makes, keeps or
shows one.

## When an app asks for it

An app opens without any setup. You can use it at once. The recovery key
comes when you first need it:

| You tap | The app says | Buttons |
|---|---|---|
| **Make a backup** | "Set up your recovery key first". A backup is locked with it. | **Create a recovery key**, **I already have one**, **Not now** |
| **App lock** | "Set up your recovery key first". The words are the way back in. | **Create a recovery key**, **I already have one**, **Not now** |
| **Restore a backup** | "Enter your recovery key first". A backup opens only with the words it was made with. | **Enter the words**, **Cancel** |

Under the reason, the first two say: "If another shkriuss app already
has your recovery key, tap I already have one and enter the same words."

If another app of the suite on this phone keeps your recovery key, the
first two and **Restore a backup** also offer **Get the keys from**
that app, for example **Get the keys from Water**. Tap it to set this
app up with no words to type
([Get the keys from another app](#get-the-keys-from-another-app)).

1. A button opens the screens below.
2. When you are done there, the app goes on with what you tapped: the
   file picker for a backup or a restore, or the app lock screen.
3. **Not now** and **Cancel** change nothing. You can go on using the
   app without a recovery key.
4. If setting up the key stopped half way, the app says "Finish your
   recovery key" instead, with **Open the security screen**.

## One key for every app

Use the same recovery key in every app of the suite. Each app is set up
on its own:

1. Create the key in the first app you set up, for example Water, and
   let the phone keep it.
2. In the next app, for example Reminders, tap **Get the keys from
   Water** when it asks for the key, or on its security screen
   ([Get the keys from another app](#get-the-keys-from-another-app)).
   You type no words.
3. Or enter the same 24 words in it: tap **I already have one** when it
   asks for the key. On its security screen, the button is **I have a
   recovery key**. The screen says so too: "If another shkriuss app
   already has your recovery key, tap I have a recovery key and enter
   the same words." Then it can keep the key too.
4. Both apps then have their keys from the same recovery key. Their
   short name is the same.

Do not create a second key in the next app. A backup opens only with the
words it was made with, so two keys mean two papers to keep.

## Get the keys from another app

An app that keeps your recovery key on this phone can give another app
of the suite its own keys. That app never gets the 24 words.

1. In the new app, tap **Get the keys from Water**. It shows on the
   security screen, and when the app first asks for the key. It shows
   only when Water keeps the key on this phone. If several apps keep a
   recovery key, each has its own button. When they keep different
   keys, each button also names its key's short name.
2. Water opens and says which app asks, and the short name of your
   recovery key. Check that both are right.
3. Tap **Allow**, and answer the system prompt. Tap **Do not allow** to
   give nothing.
4. You are back in the new app. It says **This app has its keys**, with
   the same short name. Tap **Done**.

What this means:

- The new app gets only its own keys. It cannot show the words. Its
  security screen says "The recovery key is kept in Water", with **Open
  Water**.
- Its backups open with the same 24 words, in this app on any phone.
- The same button also repairs a damaged key file and opens the files
  again after the screen lock was removed, when Water keeps the same
  recovery key.
- If the new app already has keys of another recovery key, it says so
  and changes nothing.
- Only apps of the suite signed exactly like Water get an answer. Water
  checks this itself, every time. Any other app that asks gets nothing:
  Water shows it no screen and gives it no keys.
- Update all apps of the suite together. An app that is not up to date
  may not show the button, or may say: "This app and Water are not of
  the same version." If Water refuses the new app, the new app says:
  "Water cannot give this app its keys. It is not signed like this app,
  or not up to date." Update both apps, or enter the words instead.

## What it is

1. 24 English words, in an order that matters. They are the same in
   every language of the apps.
2. **Whoever has the words can read your backups and your synced data.**
   Keep the paper where nobody else can find it.
3. **If you lose the words and your phone, nobody can help.** Not the
   people who make the apps. There is no password reset.
4. The words look like a cryptocurrency wallet phrase. They are not one,
   and they hold no money. Never type them into a wallet or a website.
5. A phone can keep a copy behind your fingerprint or your PIN, so that it
   can show you the words again. That copy is a convenience. The paper
   is the backup.

## Create a recovery key

1. Open the app's security screen and tap **Create a recovery key**. Or
   tap **Create a recovery key** when the app asks for the key
   ([When an app asks for it](#when-an-app-asks-for-it)).
2. Read the screen. Make sure nobody can see your phone. If TalkBack is
   on, it reads the words aloud.
3. Tap **Show the words**. Write the 24 words on paper, in order, with
   their numbers.
4. Tap **I wrote them down**.
5. The app asks for four of the words. Type each one and pick it from
   the list under the field. Tap **Check**.
   - If your letters start no word of the list, the field says so at
     once. Check that word on your paper.
   - If one does not match, the app shows the words again. Check your
     paper and try again.
6. The app stores its keys. Then the system prompt asks for your
   fingerprint, or your phone's PIN, pattern or password. That lets this
   phone keep the recovery key.
   - Close the prompt, or tap **Not now**, to keep only the paper copy.
     Everything else works.
7. Tap **Done**.

## Enter your recovery key

On a new phone, after the app's data was cleared, or to keep the key on
this phone again:

1. Open the app's security screen and tap **I have a recovery key**.
2. Type the first word. The words of the list that start with your
   letters show under the field. Tap the right one, or press the
   keyboard's next key once you have typed four letters.
   - If your letters start no word of the list, the field says so at
     once, and the next key does nothing. Check that word on your
     paper, and delete the letters that are wrong.
3. The next word comes next. To change a word, tap it in the list of
   numbered words.
4. When all 24 are in, tap **Check the words**.
   - "Word 7 is not in the word list": check word 7 on your paper.
   - "These words do not belong together": a word is wrong, or two are
     swapped. Check each word.
5. The app stores its keys, and asks to keep the recovery key, as when
   you create one. If another app on this phone keeps the same key, it
   first asks **Keep it here too?** Tap **Keep it here too** to keep it
   in this app as well, or **Not now**. After **Not now**, the app says
   which app keeps the key.
6. If the app lock was on and these words repaired its key file, or the
   app lock was reset because the screen lock was removed, the end of
   the screen says that the app lock is off now. Tap **App lock** to
   turn it on again ([The app lock](app-lock.md)).

You cannot paste the words, and you cannot copy or cut what you typed.
The field takes one letter at a time and shows no menu for copy and
paste, so the words never end up in the clipboard, where other apps
could read them.

## See the words again

Only a phone that keeps the recovery key can show it.

1. Open the app's security screen and tap **Show the recovery key**.
2. Tap **Show the words**, and answer the system prompt.
3. The words show once. They leave the screen when you close it, when
   the phone's screen turns off, or when you switch to another app.

If the phone says it does not keep the key any more, the screen lock may
have been removed. Enter the words once to keep it again.

## The security screen

It shows:

- Whether the app has a recovery key, and its short name: eight letters
  and digits. The short name is not secret and opens nothing. It helps
  you tell two recovery keys apart.
- Whether this phone keeps the recovery key, or which app keeps it:
  "The recovery key is kept in Water", with **Open Water**.
- Whether the phone has a screen lock. Without one, the phone cannot keep
  the recovery key or show it again, and the app lock cannot be turned
  on. Backup, sync and files still work.
- The keys that protect the app's data on this phone, and where each
  lives:
  - **StrongBox**: a separate security chip. The best.
  - **Trusted execution environment**: a secure area of the main
    processor.
  - **Software only**: not protected by hardware. Emulators are such
    phones.

  No key can be copied out of the phone.

It also says whether the app lock is on, and its button **App lock**
opens the app lock screen ([The app lock](app-lock.md)).

Screenshots and screen recordings are blocked on every one of these
screens, and the app shows blank in the list of recent apps.

## Other apps on your phone

The screens of the recovery key keep other apps away. So do the
security screen and the app lock screen.

1. **Apps that draw over other apps disappear** while one of these
   screens shows, and come back when you leave it. Examples are screen
   dimmers, chat bubbles of other apps, and the buttons of screen
   recorders. Android's own parts stay: the status bar, the keyboard,
   notifications and TalkBack. So no other app can put a fake screen
   over your words.
2. **A tap under another app's window does nothing.** If a tap seems
   to do nothing, close the app that draws over other apps, then try
   again. To find it: open Android's settings, then **Apps**, then
   **Special app access**, then **Display over other apps**.
3. **Accessibility apps.** Only apps that declare themselves
   accessibility tools can read these screens. TalkBack is such an app.
   - An assistive app that does not declare itself an accessibility tool
     cannot read these screens.
   - A harmful app can falsely claim to be an accessibility tool. Only
     turn on accessibility apps you trust.

## If setting up stopped

If the app was stopped while it stored its keys, the security screen
says that setting up stopped half way.

1. Tap **Enter the words to finish** and enter the same 24 words.
2. If you no longer have those words, tap **I do not have those words**.
   Then create a new recovery key, or enter other words. The files on
   this phone stay.

## If a key file is damaged

The security screen says when a key file of the app is damaged.

1. Tap **Enter the words to repair** and enter your 24 words. If they
   are the right ones, the app repairs the file and nothing is lost. It
   keeps the damaged file too. It never deletes it.
2. Some key files cannot be brought back by any words, for example the
   key of files you added before you had a recovery key. Then the
   security screen offers **Give up these files**. Those files can then
   never be opened again, on any device. Backup and sync work again.
   The app asks before it does this.

## Preview builds

A preview build is an app from a pull request, for testing. Never type
your real recovery words into one. It shows a warning on these screens.
Make a new recovery key in it for testing.

A preview takes part in **Get the keys from another app** only with a
preview from the same run, never with a real app. Install both previews
from the pull request's preview comment after the same push
([Test a pull request on a phone](../development/test-a-pull-request.md)).
